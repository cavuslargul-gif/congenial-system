## OctoAcme project management process summary

OctoAcme starts with initiation, where the team validates the business need, defines stakeholder ownership, documents the problem and desired outcome, sets measurable success criteria, and decides whether to move forward with planning. This stage creates a lightweight charter or one-pager to confirm alignment before detailed work begins.

Once work is approved, the team moves into planning. Backlog items are prioritized, scope is estimated, milestones are mapped, dependencies are identified, and acceptance criteria are documented. A clear Definition of Done helps the team decide when work is ready to be pulled into a sprint or release cycle. The planning process makes delivery more predictable and ensures the team is aligned on scope, sequencing, and accountability.

Execution follows an iterative delivery model with visible work tracking, daily or frequent standups, risk monitoring, and review checkpoints. Work is managed through a project board with stages such as backlog, ready, in progress, review, QA, and done. Developers work in small, reviewable pull requests, and the team relies on testing, CI, code review, and escalation paths to address blockers and quality concerns early.

Release and deployment are treated as controlled operational activities. Before shipping, the team confirms acceptance criteria, runs CI and security checks, validates smoke tests, and prepares release notes and rollback plans. Post-deployment verification and stakeholder communication reduce risk during rollout and help teams respond quickly if an issue occurs. Retrospectives close the loop by capturing what went well, what could improve, and which action items should be tracked into the backlog or follow-up work.

This documentation set gives OctoAcme a repeatable operating model that balances business alignment, cross-functional ownership, risk management, quality assurance, and continuous improvement.
